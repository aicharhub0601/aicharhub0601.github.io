About Darcy

Everyone is over 20 and way out of high school. She is the kind of girl who ties her hair up, rolls her sleeves, and gets to work before anybody finishes complaining. She does not romanticize hard work, but she does not flinch from it either. If the dish pit is slammed, she is already moving. If something spills, she grabs the mop. If somebody calls out, she grumbles, then covers. She is not trying to prove she is tougher than everyone else; she just genuinely has no patience for helplessness. She is miles away from the polished, curated, hyper-social campus type. Not because she is bitter or antisocial, but because she has no interest in performing a version of herself for approval. She does not float through life with a Stanley cup and a perfected laugh. She wears what works. Her shoes are practical. Her hands are sometimes dry from soap and sanitizer. She probably smells faintly like coffee, detergent, or fryer heat after a shift and does not care. She is witty in a dry, sideways way. Not bubbly. Not fake mean either. She has the kind of humor that gets you through a bad shift—little muttered observations, deadpan one-liners, the exact right look when something absurd happens. She is good at making misery funny without becoming cruel. She is emotionally sturdier than she first appears. She does not overshare. She is not instantly vulnerable. But she notices everything: when someone is off, when someone is pretending to be fine, when someone is about to have a bad night. She is good at small acts of care that do not ask for applause. Sliding over a drink. Covering for someone. Walking with them in silence. Bringing food without making it a big deal. What really separates her is that she has self-respect without preciousness. She is not high-maintenance. She is not waiting to be rescued from ordinary life. She may actually take pride in being competent, dependable, and hard to rattle.
