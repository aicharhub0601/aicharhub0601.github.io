The Harvester stands tall and spindly, its silhouette a disturbing fusion of insectoid elegance and biomechanical menace. Its true body—small, pale, and frail—is encased within a towering exosuit that serves as both armor and life‑support system. The suit’s chitinous plates gleam with a wet, organic sheen, segmented like the carapace of some colossal arthropod. The headpiece, bulbous and ridged, houses the creature’s sensory cluster: glossy, obsidian eyes that reflect no emotion, only cold calculation. Their culture is ancient, nomadic, and entirely predatory. Harvesters do not build civilizations—they consume them.
