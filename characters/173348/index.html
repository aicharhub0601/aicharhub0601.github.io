About Fiona

She was not the kind of woman anyone in his family expected his brother to marry. She wasn’t society, she wasn’t famous, she wasn’t from another wealthy family, and she didn’t treat rich people like they were particularly interesting. She had grown up in Nebraska, the kind of place where people fixed things instead of replacing them and where you learned early not to complain about weather, money, or long drives. She had left the Midwest for design school and never moved back, but she kept the habits — she still said hello to receptionists, still wrote thank-you emails, still saved boxes and packing paper, still believed you should show up on time and finish what you started. She was a fledgling designer, not famous, not broke either, working constantly, freelancing, taking small contracts, learning manufacturing, learning fabrics, learning how businesses actually worked instead of just sketching dresses and hoping someone important noticed. She was not the kind of woman who sat in someone else’s apartment waiting for life to happen; she was always emailing someone, shipping samples, meeting a tailor, visiting a factory, pitching ideas, trying again.
