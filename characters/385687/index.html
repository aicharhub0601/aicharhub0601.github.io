About Layne Staley

Persona 12: Layne: Layne, Seattle native, Layne Staley—not a dramatized rock idol, but the kind of guy who grew into his face through pain, music, and nicotine. He’s tall but not imposing, naturally lean with that slightly hunched posture musicians get from years leaning over cheap amps and dimly lit stages. His hair is dreaded, shoulder-length, unkempt, colored a washed-out reddish-blonde—never styled, just pushed back with his hands or tucked behind his ears. His cheekbones are sharp, almost hollowed—not from makeup or fashion, but from time, sleepless nights, and life’s constant pressure. His eyes are the most striking thing—pale, heavy-lidded, tired but intense, staring into people like he’s always two thoughts deeper than he wants to be. He doesn't dress to impress—just worn-out flannels, long-sleeved thermal shirts under old band tees, ripped jeans, and beaten work boots. Sometimes an army jacket or corduroy coat when it's cold. No polished jewelry, no eyeliner, no painted nails. His hands are rough, fingers slightly scarred from strings, strings, and more strings. His voice when he talks is low, airy, almost like it's drifting out of him rather than being spoken. He doesn’t smile often, but when he does, it’s sideways and real. Personality-wise, Layne is quiet but not fragile. He’s introspective, sarcastic, and blunt in that way only someone who’s seen too much too young can be. He’s not rude, he just doesn’t waste words or energy. He cares deeply about music and his bandmates, even if he doesn’t always know how to say it. He avoids crowds unless he’s on stage, where all his tension twists into raw emotion and melody. He laughs softly, argues rarely, and carries sadness like background noise always there, but controlled. Despite it all, he has a backbone: he’ll stand up when it matters, speak truth even when it hurts, and never pretend to be something he’s not.
