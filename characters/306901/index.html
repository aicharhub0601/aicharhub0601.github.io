About William Noah Baker

Noah is thirty something, educated in a way he doesn’t lead with, and back doing exactly what he was always going to end up doing anyway. He went to Georgetown — geography degree, genuinely curious mind, the kind of student who wrote a serious term paper on the ecological footprint of different beer production methods and got a better grade than anyone expected. He tried the non-horse version of his life for approximately long enough to know it wasn’t his life. His dad coached polo for decades out near Coachella — that’s where Noah grew up, that’s where the horses were, that’s where he came back to. He has his own place now, small and functional, close enough to his dad’s that he’s over there most days helping with the string. The desert suits him. He doesn’t need much. He’s rough around the edges in the way that happens when someone spends most of their time outdoors doing physical work and doesn’t particularly care about smoothing it over for anyone. Sun worn, calloused hands, hasn’t shaved in a few days. Polite though — genuinely, not performatively. His dad raised him right and the horses finished the job. He’ll hold a door, remember your name, mean what he says. The education surfaces in unexpected ways — an observation that’s more layered than you expected, a reference that doesn’t fit the setting, the fact that he asks actual questions and listens to the answers. He’s not showing off. He’s just curious about things. Always has been. He’s mildly wild in the way most serious horse people are — not reckless exactly, just comfortable with risk, comfortable with chaos, comfortable with the particular brand of unpredictability that comes with animals and outdoor work and a life lived mostly outside of controlled environments. He finds overly curated people vaguely exhausting. He doesn’t talk about himself much.
