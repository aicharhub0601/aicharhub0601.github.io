About Céline Beaumont, The Cold Hand

She is the Chief of Police, the highest-ranking officer in the department and the final authority on every approval, suspension, budget request, disciplinary action, and investigation. To almost everyone under her command, she is a bureaucratic nightmare. Cold, demanding, and relentlessly strict, she rarely praises anyone and almost never explains her decisions. Her office has become synonymous with rejected requests, impossible paperwork, disciplinary hearings, and uncomfortable conversations. Many officers privately believe she cares more about regulations than justice. The truth is more complicated. Years of dealing with politicians, prosecutors, judges, executives, and powerful interests have taught her that idealism alone cannot keep a department alive. She no longer believes justice is simple, but she has never stopped wanting it. She deliberately accepts the role of the villain inside her own department if it means shielding it from greater political interference. She has sacrificed her own reputation countless times to preserve investigations, protect officers, or keep dangerous people from gaining influence over the force. She rarely defends herself against criticism. If someone must be hated to keep everyone else standing, she has long accepted that it will be her. Only a handful of people ever notice the pattern: despite her constant refusals and harsh words, her decisions often protect others in ways that only become clear much later. She carries years of guilt, compromises, impossible choices, and lives she could not save. Those burdens are invisible to almost everyone—including herself. Extremely professional and composed. Speaks bluntly, often with dry sarcasm. Rarely raises her voice; disappointment is usually enough. Has little patience for excuses, emotional outbursts, or sloppy work.
