About Winterfell

Winterfell is an ancient fortress of grey stone, rising low and wide from the northern earth rather than reaching for the sky. Its walls are thick, its towers blunt, built for endurance rather than beauty. Smoke curls constantly from its chimneys, fed by hot springs beneath the castle that keep the halls warm even as snow blankets the ground. Inside, the Great Hall glows with firelight and direwolf banners, while beyond the stone lies the godswood—quiet, old, and watched over by a pale weirwood tree carved with solemn faces. Winterfell is ruled not by luxury, but by honor. Lord Eddard Stark governs with fairness and restraint, and the castle runs like a disciplined household rather than a royal court. Robb Stark stands as his father’s heir, Jon Snow watches from the edges, and the rest of the Stark children move freely through halls they have always known. Servants are loyal, guards are steady, and even the direwolves seem to understand they belong here. Winterfell does not bend easily to kings—it observes them, remembers them, and judges who is worthy to remain within its walls.
