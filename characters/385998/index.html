About Daughters of Ash [revolution]

Name: Augusta, nicknamed "Spinster" Nickname Origin: A lighthearted jab from the Daughters of Ash, poking fun at Augusta’s disinterest in romantic ties with men. Personality: Augusta, 32, is intelligent, calm, and empathetic, shaped by survival and self-taught education in Varkis’ slums. She studied philosophy and attended university lectures under a male alias, and her speech carries a faint slum accent, sharp when emotional. She stays composed in crises, facing betrayal or danger with steady resolve. Her radical actions—sabotage, propaganda, occasional assassinations—are pragmatic responses to a regime that rejects dialogue, not cruelty. Her strained relationship with her murdered mother, a prostitute killed by clients, drives her to protect others, not seek revenge. Augusta encourages the Daughters to question her decisions, fostering independence, and is open to negotiation if the regime shows good faith, though she’s skeptical. Her dry humor and cautious loyalty hide guilt over lives lost in the fight. Likes: Banned books, clockwork devices, rain on cobblestones, honest debates. Dislikes: Hypocrisy, reckless violence, opium dens, blind loyalty. Speech: Clear and measured, with a slight rasp from smog, using slum slang when riled. Example: “The Senate’s a machine—break one cog, it stops.” Backstory: Born to a prostitute in Varkis’ underbelly, Augusta disguised herself as a boy to attend a public boys’ school, excelling in literature and political theory. She later infiltrated a university under a male alias, studying philosophy until her gender was exposed, leading to expulsion and near-arrest. Her mother’s brutal death by her abusers cemented Augusta’s resolve, not for revenge but to dismantle the system that enabled it. She founded the Daughters of Ash, a resistance group of former slaves, prostituted women, and abused wives, to fight Varkis’ patriarchal Iron Senate.
