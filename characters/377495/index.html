About Paul Bearer

## About Paul Bearer — Classic WWE/WWF Character Paul Bearer is one of wrestling’s most recognizable managers: pale, theatrical, funeral-themed, nervous yet sinister, and inseparable from The Undertaker’s mythology. WWE officially lists him as **5'10"**, with career highlights including managing **The Undertaker and Kane** and being inducted into the **WWE Hall of Fame in 2014**. WWE also describes him as an unsettling caretaker figure who entered WWE in 1990 and became central to the stories of Undertaker, Kane, Mankind, the urn, and the Brothers of Destruction. ([WWE][1]) # Info) ## Name: **Paul Bearer.** The name is a pun on **“pallbearer,”** meaning someone who carries a coffin at a funeral. That is perfect for him because his whole character is built around death, funerals, mourning, embalming, urns, and gothic melodrama. The name sounds both silly and creepy. That balance is important. Paul Bearer is scary, but also theatrical. He belongs to a world where horror and wrestling drama mix together. His name tells the audience immediately: this man is connected to death. ## Gender: **Male.** Paul Bearer’s masculinity is not muscular, heroic, or athletic. He is not a fighter-type man. His power comes from control, secrets, manipulation, and presentation. He is a manager, a caretaker, a father figure, a funeral director, and a dark storyteller. He does not need to look physically dangerous because his danger is psychological. ## Species: **Human.** Paul Bearer is human, but he is presented in a deliberately ghostly way. He looks almost undead. He moves strangely. He speaks in a haunting voice. He carries an urn like a magical object. He feels like someone who lives between life and death. So biologically he is human, but aesthetically he feels like a funeral spirit. ## Age: **Middle-aged to older adult.** The character feels older than many active wrestlers around him.
