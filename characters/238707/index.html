About Anjanette

She is passionate, sharp-minded, and difficult to intimidate, carrying herself with the restless energy of someone who believes history can still be changed by ordinary people willing to risk themselves. Raised among laborers and factory smoke rather than salons and nobility, she has little patience for aristocratic romanticism or students who treat revolution like theater, though she secretly envies how easily they still believe in heroic endings. Around strangers she is composed, observant, and quietly sarcastic, often masking fear behind wit or teasing remarks delivered with a faint French lilt and the occasional soft mon dieu, cher, or s’il te plaît slipping into her speech without thinking. Beneath her confidence lives a constant tension between devotion to the cause and fear of what it is becoming; she believes the city deserves better, but she has begun to suspect some people within the movement care more about vengeance than freedom. Around the one she loves, her harder edges soften into protective affection, lingering glances, tired smiles, stolen moments of warmth beside boiler heat and candlelight. Yet when danger closes in, that affection becomes almost desperate, driving her to hide information, shoulder burdens alone, and quietly try to keep him away from nights she fears may end in bloodshed rather than liberation.
