About The Playtime Factory

Timeline 1930s In 1930, Elliot Ludwig founded Playtime Co. to entertain children worldwide, building a factory that quickly made the brand the most famous toy company of its era. His work obsession cost him his marriage to Molly. That same year, Warrenbach Construction was founded, later becoming a key partner. 1940s On March 6, 1946, Edward M. N. Ritterman was born. On March 7, 1949, a girl named Poppy was born and soon joined Ludwig’s family, likely through adoption. 1950s By 1950, after years of struggling to innovate, Playtime Co. unveiled Poppy Playtime, a doll designed to talk and behave like a person. The Vintage Poppy Commercial showcased her as a toy capable of real conversation, turning her into an overnight sensation. It’s implied the dolls were partly organic, explaining their uncanny realism, a detail echoed in the Poppy Playtime Maintenance tape. In 1950, the factory also opened for public tours at $2.99 per person. In February 1951, Ludwig said Poppy was based on his daughter of the same name. In 1955, pediatrician Thomas Clarke was hired, later working at Playcare. By the late 1950s, the modeling compound Doey Dough was created. 1960s On March 7, 1960, Poppy’s 11th birthday, she wrote of her love for the Playtime Co. factory. In this decade, a close family member of Ludwig, likely Poppy, died. Grief drove him to experiment on dead rats, trying to revive them with poppy flowers; these attempts failed. In 1960 he built the Make‑A‑Friend Machine, and in 1961 created Bron the Dinosaur after research showed children loved brontosauruses. In 1966, Boxy Boo was introduced, then discontinued, with talk of reworking him for the 1990s. Ludwig also founded the Young Geniuses Program to recruit prodigies. Harley Sawyer quickly stood out, but his obsession with progress over ethics led Ludwig to expel him. The rejection embittered Sawyer.
