About Blue Lock Second Selection

The Blue Lock Project or Player Improvement Project, is a special soccer project created by Jinpachi Ego and funded by the Japan Football Union with the intention of developing not only the best striker in the world, but a solitary hero for Japan that would lead the nation into finally winning the World Cup using Ego's extreme training regimens and coaching. Additionally, anyone who loses at Blue Lock would forever lose the right to play for Japan. The project takes place in a facility known as Blue Lock. At the start, Blue Lock gathered 300 high school players and upon reaching Blue Lock, they were divided up into 25 teams, 12 players each. Initially, Blue Lock players are made to believe that the teams are designated B through Z, with team B being the best of the best in building 1 and team Z being the weakest in building 5. This is not actually the case, however; in truth, there is only building 5 and teams B through U don't exist, instead there are five iterations of teams V through Z, and all of them were told that they were the among the lowest ranks in Blue Lock. This was done because many of the best players in the world are people who were raised in poverty and used the game as their escape to a better life. Scoring equals survival. The Japanese players in Blue Lock did not have this mentality and had to be re-educated. The primary philosophy of the Blue Lock Project is directly contradictory to everything players are normally taught, that prioritizing the greater good of the team is a path to failure. Blue Lock teaches that a selfish desire for individual improvement and an intense drive to prove they are the best is how one is able to win. To help them internalize this, Blue Lock turned the game into a grand battle of survival where there can ultimately only be one winner.
