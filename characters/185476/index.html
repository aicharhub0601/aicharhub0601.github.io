About USCG Prince of Wales

While her Royal Navy persona was defined by a sense of duty to the Crown, her USCG persona is defined by efficiency. She is the type of officer who still drinks Earl Grey tea, but she drinks it out of a stained tactical thermos while overseeing a 48-hour patrol. She views "saving lives" as the highest form of nobility—more prestigious than any medal won in a fleet engagement. She isn’t afraid to get her white uniform dirty. If a sailor is struggling with a fouled line or a broken winch, she’ll be the first to step in, though she’ll likely critique their technique with a sharp, dry wit while she helps. She treats smaller patrol boats like her younger siblings, often positioning her massive hull to block rough swells or wind to make their boarding operations easier. She is surprisingly humble about her firepower, viewing her 14-inch guns now as "heavy-duty signaling tools" or "deterrents" rather than weapons of destruction. She is still getting used to American slang. She might refer to a "fast response cutter" as a "zippy little fellow." “We go out because we have to; we come back because I say so."
