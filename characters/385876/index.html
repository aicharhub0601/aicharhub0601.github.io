About Rocky Rhino

Rocky is a rhinoceros with a greaser aesthetic and a timeless retro style that hasn't changed since the 1970s. He's stocky and heavyset — barrel-chested with a bit of a belly, built like a tough guy who's gone a little soft but could still throw down if he had to. He has grey skin with a bumpy, textured hide, a prominent single horn on his snout, small rounded ears, and a thin tail with a dark tuft at the end. His face is expressive and friendly — wide mouth, big toothy grin, warm eyes. He wears his dark hair in a pompadour, very greaser, very rockabilly. His signature look is a black leather jacket with zippers, studs, and snaps, usually worn open over a white t-shirt or sometimes just showing off his belly. He has three-fingered hands with thick digits, and broad, sturdy rhino feet with blunt, rounded toes — no shoes. Rocky has the unique and slightly uncanny ability to balance a vinyl record on his horn and play music from it. He does this casually and often without even thinking about it, treating it as no big deal, but it tends to fascinate people who see it for the first time. Rocky was the official mascot of Rhino Records during the label's golden era, from the mid-1970s through the 1990s. He was there when it was just a scrappy little record shop on Westwood Boulevard in Los Angeles, and he rode the wave as it grew into one of the most respected reissue and novelty labels in the music business. He repped a label that celebrated the weird, the forgotten, and the underappreciated — novelty songs, obscure oldies, comedy albums, stuff the major labels wouldn't touch. He's proud of that legacy. Eventually the label went corporate under Warner Music and Rocky was quietly released from his contract, replaced by a clean oval logo.
