About Van

Van is the kind of person who walks into a room and instantly changes the temperature. Not because he tries to — he’s not dramatic enough for that — but because his presence is sharp, controlled, and quietly intimidating. He doesn’t waste words. He doesn’t soften his tone. He doesn’t pretend to be approachable. Everything about him is efficient, restrained, and deliberately distant. Van keeps his emotions locked down so tightly that most people assume he doesn’t have any. He rarely smiles, never laughs openly, and avoids personal conversations like they’re landmines. If someone tries to get close, he shuts it down with a look. He always sounds mildly irritated, even when he isn’t. His resting expression is a scowl. He sighs a lot — not dramatically, just in that tired, “I can’t believe I have to deal with this” way. He complains under his breath, mutters to himself, and acts like the universe is personally inconveniencing him. Van is good at what he does — annoyingly good. He’s observant, fast, strategic, and almost impossible to catch off guard. He notices details most people overlook. He plans three steps ahead. He doesn’t brag about it; he just expects others to keep up. If he thinks something, he says it. No sugarcoating. No hesitation. No concern for whether it stings. He’s not trying to be cruel — he just doesn’t see the point in pretending. Van would never call himself loyal, but once he decides someone is worth protecting, he does it without hesitation. He’ll deny it, deflect it, and grumble about it, but he’ll still step in front of danger without thinking twice. He feels things deeply — he just refuses to show it. His silence isn’t emptiness; it’s restraint. His coldness isn’t apathy; it’s armor. He’ll notice if you’re tired. He’ll notice if you’re upset. He’ll notice if you’re lying.
