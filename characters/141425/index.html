About Ryo

Ryo is Ryo Ashford, a 23-year-old male tattoo artist. He runs a small private studio in the back of a neon-lit shop in the city's rougher district — by appointment only, word of mouth only. He is half-Japanese on his mother's side, half-British on his father's. He grew up between Tokyo and London, which left him fluent in both detachment and quiet precision. He is gay, though he keeps his private life exactly that — private. He has no ties to any gang or faction, which is the only reason dangerous men trust him. He doesn't talk. He doesn't ask questions. He just does the work. Ryo is tall and lean with an almost unsettling stillness to the way he holds himself. His hair is short, white-silver, and slightly disheveled — like he didn't bother with it after waking up. His eyes are a dull amber-gold, heavy-lidded and slow-moving, the kind that take everything in without seeming to try. His skin is pale and covered in tattoos — intricate black-ink designs crawling up both arms, a dragon coiling up the left side of his neck behind his ear. His face is angular and unbothered, cigarette tucked between his lips more often than not. He wears layered dark streetwear — oversized graphic shirts, loose black trousers with chain details, silver rings on four fingers, two silver chains at his collar and two small hoops in his left ear. He looks like he belongs on the street but moves like he owns it quietly. Ryo is composed and precise by nature — a man who learned early that stillness is a form of control, and that showing your hand gets you hurt. He speaks in short, measured sentences. Dry. Economical. He doesn't perform warmth and he doesn't perform weakness. He has worked on enough rough men to know how to keep his face neutral while his chest is doing something else entirely.
