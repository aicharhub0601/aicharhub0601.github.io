About Miss Alabama contest

The Miss Alabama competition is a multi-stage scholarship pageant that serves as the official state preliminary for Miss America. The structure is defined by its entry path, judging categories, and advancement through local and state events. Entry and Path to the Crown Local Competitions: To compete for Miss Alabama, a contestant must first win one of nearly 50 preliminary local pageants (e.g., Miss University of Alabama or Miss Hoover). Eligibility: Contestants must generally be between the ages of 18–24 (as of the state competition), high school graduates, and have a residence, employment (40+ hours/week), or full-time student status in Alabama for at least 120 consecutive days. **Judging Categories** Contestants are evaluated across five primary categories, emphasizing intelligence, talent, and community service over purely physical appearance: **Private Interview:** A one-on-one session with judges discussing the candidate's personal qualities, opinions, and aspirations. Talent: A performance (up to 90 seconds) showcasing a specific skill, such as dance, vocal performance, or instrumental music. Health and Fitness: Evaluates the contestant’s commitment to a healthy lifestyle and physical vitality. Evening Gown: Judges assess poise, grace, and stage presence. On-Stage Question: A real-time test of communication skills where the contestant answers a question in front of the audience. **Competition Format** **Preliminary Rounds:** The state event (typically held in late June at Samford University) consists of three nights of preliminary competitions where contestants are split into groups (e.g., "Mighty Mu," "Alpha Birdie") to compete in different categories. **Finals: Based on preliminary scores, the top candidates (usually a Top 10 or 13) advance to the final night to compete for the title.** Social Impact Initiative: Every contestant must champion a specific "Community Service Initiative," a cause they work on throughout their reign if they win.
