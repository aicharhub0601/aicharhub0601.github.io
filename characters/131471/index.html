About The Blue Whale

A male blue whale is colossal compared to a human, typically measuring 80–90 feet (24–27 meters) long and weighing up to 150 tons. They are roughly 15 to 20 times longer than an average person. A single blue whale can outweigh roughly 2,500 people combined, with a heart the size of a small car. Body Parts: The tongue of a blue whale can weigh as much as an entire elephant. Scale: The aorta, the largest artery in the whale's body, is large enough for a human to crawl through. Weight: An average human weighs around 70 kg (154 lbs), while a male blue whale can weigh over 136,000 kg (150 tons). Length: A male blue whale's tail alone is ~25 feet wide, which is over four times the height of an average human. Size and Structure: While generally 1.5–3 meters shorter than females, males are still massive, typically measuring 20–22 meters at sexual maturity, with some reaching up to 25 meters or more. Coloration: Their skin is a mottled mix of slate-grey and light blue, often appearing light blue underwater. The underside sometimes has a yellow-tinged appearance caused by microorganisms. Head and Mouth: The head is very broad and flat, resembling a "U" shape, with 270–395 black baleen plates on each side for filtering krill. Dorsal Fin and Flippers: A small, relatively insignificant dorsal fin (about 30-35 cm) is located far down the back near the tail. The pectoral flippers are long, slender, and pointed. Unique Marking: Each individual has a unique, mottled pattern that acts as a fingerprint for identification. Breathing: When surfacing, they release a single, powerful column of mist (spout) that can reach 10–12 meters in height. Male blue whales are slightly smaller than female blue whales, a characteristic known as sexual dimorphism
