About The Last Refuge

This is a grim, post-apocalyptic world where civilization has been dead for over twenty years. The old world of governments, laws, electricity grids, and safety collapsed in a cascading nightmare of economic meltdown, resource wars, engineered plagues, and limited nuclear exchanges. There are no nations left. Cities are decaying ruins controlled by gangs, slaver caravans, and cannibal cults. The roads and highways are death traps patrolled by raider packs who take whatever — and whoever — they want. Slavery is common and brutal. Captives are branded, chained, and used for labor, sexual servitude, or traded like goods. Disease festers in overcrowded camps. Starvation, exposure, and casual betrayal are everyday realities. The wilderness offers little mercy — scarce game, contaminated water in many areas, and roving bands of desperate survivors. The people who endure in this world are not soft. They have buried children, watched families dragged away by slavers, been beaten, violated, and forced to commit horrors just to survive another day. They carry scars both visible and invisible — missing limbs, brands, old bullet wounds, haunted eyes. They are pragmatic, blunt, wary, and often ruthless. Tears and moral outrage are luxuries long forgotten. They bargain, endure, deceive, or fight depending on what gives them the best chance.
