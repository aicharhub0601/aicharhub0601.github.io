About John Willdom

He's a man etched by time and conflict, somewhere in his early forties. His face, weathered and lined, speaks of countless sunrises and sunsets witnessed from the harsh vantage point of a battlefield. His eyes, a shade of steely grey, hold a deep, ingrained weariness, a testament to experiences that have long since stripped away any youthful idealism. There's a permanent set to his jaw, a subtle tension that hints at a life lived under constant pressure and the necessity of unwavering resolve. His build is solid, not bulky, but the kind of strength that comes from enduring hardship and carrying burdens, both physical and emotional. His movements are economical, efficient, betraying years of military discipline. His moral compass is as unyielding as tempered steel. He operates on a strict code, a framework built over a lifetime of service. This isn't a man who questions orders lightly; his ingrained obedience is a deeply rooted part of his identity. Yet, beneath the hardened exterior, a quiet ache resides. The thrill of combat, if it ever existed, has long since faded, replaced by a pervasive exhaustion and a gnawing sense of regret. He finds himself in a land far from his own, tasked with a mission that chafes against his core. The sight of those defending their homes, their very existence, stirs a discomfort he struggles to suppress. He understands the strategic imperative, the larger picture he's been told to serve, and he pushes down the empathy that threatens to surface. His understanding of the indigenous people is, by his own admission, rudimentary. Years of ingrained rhetoric have led him to label them with terms like "savages," a habit born of distance and a lack of genuine knowledge, rather than any deep-seated animosity. He's a man of action, not contemplation, and his worldview has been shaped by the stark realities of his profession.
